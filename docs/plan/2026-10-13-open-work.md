The app runs on the graph engine. A layout is a piece-joint graph, closure is
exact in Z[zeta16] with no epsilon anywhere, free-end clicking is the whole
editing interaction, and the chain engine survives only as a differential
reference. The verdict is announced to screen readers, failing welds are
annotated with their gap in millimetres, and the design system is named.

be edited without a mouse, or be trusted on any dimension nobody measured. The
graph model itself already expresses a branch and is tested: `switches.test.ts`
declares a switch locally and proves a 3-port piece the chain model could not

**Decision 5 — the canvas hit targets and the hover table.**

Today's measured values, all SVG user units, which are millimetres here:
free-end hit circle `r=26` (`TrackCanvas.tsx:237`), port dot `r=8`, armed ring
`r=16`, clash ring `r=28`.

U5 adds a second, semantically OPPOSITE target (destroy) into pixels next to the
existing one (create). So:

```
  TARGET      HIT r   VISIBLE        PRECEDENCE
  ----------- ------- -------------- ----------------------------------
  free end     26mm   dot r=8        WINS on overlap. Creation is the
                                     primary verb and must stay easy
  weld         14mm   see below      loses to any free end whose r=26
                                     circle covers the pointer
  clash mark    0mm   ring r=28      not a target, never was
```

**Free ends win**, deliberately: the destructive target should be the harder one to
hit. A weld inside a free end's 26mm circle is unclickable, which is correct — a
weld that close to a free end is a weld the user can break by clicking the weld's
own uncovered area, or after placing the piece they are mid-way through placing.

Hover table. Distinct by SHAPE or STROKE, never by colour alone:

```
  STATE               MARK                              CURSOR
  ------------------- --------------------------------- ---------
  free end, idle      dot r=8                           pointer
  free end, hover     dot r=8 + ring r=16, 1mm stroke   pointer
  free end, armed     dot r=8 + ring r=16, 2mm stroke   pointer
  weld, idle          nothing. A join is not a mark      default
  weld, hover         an open bracket pair at r=14,     pointer
                      the "about to come apart" shape
  weld, breaking      no transient state: the click is  pointer
                      the commit, and Undo is the path back
```

The weld hover is a bracket pair rather than a ring because every ring on this
canvas is already taken: r=16 armed, r=28 clash. A third ring would read as a third
state of the same kind.

**The verdict transition is a user-visible consequence, stated.** Breaking an
interior weld can flip `EXACT`/`verdict-pass` to `NO_CYCLE` in one unconfirmed
click. The two newly freed ends must render in the free-end affordance in the same
paint, so the green turning grey has a visible cause at the point of the click.

Undo control: dynamic label ("Undo break" / "Undo last piece" / "Undo" when
disabled), `disabled` plus `aria-disabled` whenever `lastAction === null`, which is
also the first-paint state, so the control never ships live-but-dead.

**E1 (P1, correctness) — the mount-time save destroys the user's blob before any
notice can be read, and T5 as written does not reach it.**
`App.tsx:187-189` is `useEffect(() => { saveGraph(graph); }, [graph])` with no
first-render guard. React runs it after the first paint, so the sanitized graph
is written back to storage immediately. `layoutStore.ts:51-93` returns
`EMPTY_GRAPH` on every whole-layout discard and `:98-109` silently `continue`s
past every bad anchor. So on a damaged load the original blob is overwritten with
an empty payload, or with the anchor-stripped graph, **before the user has done
anything at all**. T5's "re-save trap" is premised on the first save arriving
one interaction later; it does not. Found independently by this review and by the
native voice. Prime Directive 1's exact failure, live in shipped code, inside the
task that exists to prevent it.
**Disposition: ACCEPT into T5, and raise T5 from P2 to P1.** T5 copies the raw
blob to a rescue key whenever `discarded` is set or a dropped-anchor count is
non-zero, and suppresses the mount save until the notice is dismissed or the load
was clean. Verify: after a damaged load the original blob is still readable from
storage.

**E2 (P1, correctness) — T5's frozen notice copy is false against the code, and
the real loss is worse than the one it describes.**
`layoutStore.ts:98-109` drops **anchors**, not pieces. Every piece survives into
`out` and is returned at `:111`. So Y1 decision 3's string — *"Loaded 12 of 14
pieces. Two pieces had unreadable positions and were left out."* — is wrong on
both halves: nothing is left out, and nothing is missing. What actually happens
is worse: `frames.ts:82` is `frames.set(id, anchors.get(id) ?? IDENTITY)`, so a
component root that lost its anchor is placed at the identity. In a multi-root
forest two components then stack at the origin, `findVertexClashes` reports
clashes the saved layout never had, and the verdict flips to IMPOSSIBLE for a
reason that is a loader artifact. A notice saying "two pieces were left out"
while the canvas shows every piece and the verdict says "can't be built" is
worse than silence.
**Disposition: ACCEPT.** Y1 decision 3 is re-opened on this one point and the
copy is rewritten to name the real loss: *"Loaded all 14 pieces, but two had
unreadable positions, so part of your track may be in the wrong place."*
T5 gains a verify line: a dropped root anchor on a two-component layout must not
produce a clash verdict, and the notice must say position, not omission.

**E3 (P1, correctness) — U5's undo reconstructs forward instead of restoring, and
anchors are the state it loses.**
`removeWeld` (`edit.ts:47-53`) rewrites `anchors` through `captureAnchors`;
`weldEnds` (`layoutModel.ts:129-133`) returns `{...graph, welds: [...]}` and
touches anchors not at all. `weldEnds(a,b)` is therefore not the inverse of
`removeWeld(i)`. And `captureAnchors:37` is `if (anchors.has(root)) continue` —
deliberate, and tested as "an anchor already written is never rewritten" — so the
break→undo→place→break flow in U5's own verify list reuses an anchor captured
under the pre-undo topology and puts the freed component at a stale frame.
`frames.ts:82` reads anchors only for forest roots, so the leaked entry stays
invisible until that piece is a root again, and `saveGraph` persists it. U5's
verify ("Undo immediately after a break re-welds those exact two ports") passes
with all of this true.
**Disposition: ACCEPT the native voice's fix.** `lastAction` carries the previous
`Graph` value, not a semantic `{kind:'break', a, b}` descriptor. `Graph` is
immutable and structurally shared, so the snapshot is a handful of pointers and
undo is exactly inverse by construction for both verbs. Verify: after
break→undo, `graph.anchors` deep-equals the pre-break map; after
break→undo→place→break the freed component sits where it was drawn.

**E4 (P1, correctness) — the break action must not carry a weld index.**
`removeWeld` filters positionally (`edit.ts:50`) and `weldEnds` re-appends, so
breaking permutes weld order — and `CycleReport.welds` (`cycles.ts:49-50`) is a
list of weld **indices**. U5 wires `TrackCanvas` into `editorReducer` without
saying what the action carries. An index computed from a render snapshot and
applied to a newer graph breaks the wrong weld: the identical defect the plan
already fixed for `add`'s `count` in T4. W2's gap annotations are index-keyed
too, so they re-map after break-then-undo.
**Disposition: ACCEPT.** The action carries `{a, b}` PortRefs and the reducer
resolves the index itself, mirroring T4's boundary decision. Verify: breaking
weld 0 of three leaves the other two identifiable by ports, not positions.

**E5 (P1, scope) — `app/src/verdictAction.ts` is invisible to the entire plan,
and Y1 decision 1 silently reverses design D10.**
The file exists, has its own passing 5-test file, and exports
`ACTION_FOR_VERDICT` over `Extract<VerdictKind, 'IMPOSSIBLE'|'EXACT'|'NEEDS_GIVE'|'EXCEEDS'>`
(`:32-33`), `ACTION_FOR_FAILURE` over the four failure kinds (`:43-47`), and
`ACTION_WHEN_EMPTY = null` (`:59`) whose docblock reads: *"No action on a blank
canvas. The empty state already carries the one primary action, and a second
invitation here is the exact thing design D10 removed."* `VerdictLine` renders
`<Action>` in every branch. The file appears in **no** task's file list — not
X2's, not T3a's, not Y1's. Two consequences. First, X2's novelty is overstated:
compile-time exhaustiveness over `VerdictKind` already exists twice here, so what
X2 actually adds is tone and ARIA, not the `Record` pattern. Second, Y1 decision 1
and the state table both require the empty state to render "INSTRUCTION, own
token" in the verdict — which is the second invitation D10 removed. D10 sits in
the plan's NOT-in-scope list, where re-opening one "needs a reason, not a review
pass". The plan reopens it in a file it never names. This is the one place the
checked-citation discipline lapsed, and it lapsed into a reversal of a closed
decision.
**Disposition: ACCEPT.** `verdictAction.ts` joins Y1's inputs and X2b's file
list. Y1 must rule explicitly on whether the empty-state instruction lives in the
verdict region (reversing D10, with the reason written down) or on the canvas
only — the state table's own row 1 already puts a preset invitation on the
canvas, which may make the verdict instruction redundant and D10 correct.

**Disposition: ACCEPT.** Add a wrong-length `pos`, a non-finite `pos` entry and

#### Why discreteness is the argument, corrected

The BRIO straights are 54, 72, 108, 144 and 216mm, so swapping one BRIO
straight for another changes a loop by 18, 36, 54, 72, 90, 108, 144 or 162mm.
Against that, `axialMm` is 0.8mm per part (`engine/data/brio.ts:31`) and
`totalGiveMm` sums one per joint (`engine/src/solver/residual.ts:135`), so a
ten-joint loop has 8.0mm of give in total.

**That 18mm figure is a property of the BRIO rows only, not of the shipped
catalogue.** `BUILT_IN` is `[...BRIO_PARTS, ...THIRD_PARTY_SEEDS]`
(`engine/data/library.ts:42`), which adds straights at 30, 60, 110, 150 and
200mm. A1 at 108mm to PLAYTIVE-110 at 110mm is a **2mm** swap, and
`brandFit('BRIO', 'PLAYTIVE')` is `'unknown'`, not `'does-not-join'`
(`engine/data/thirdParty.ts:162-164`). `SubmitPart.tsx` also accepts a
straight of any length. So the length set is not closed and 2mm sits well
inside an 8mm budget.

**Withdrawn:** the claim that `NEEDS_GIVE` cannot be reached by swapping
pieces. Repair starts from a gap G, and a swap of delta d leaves near
|G - d|, which lands in the band whenever G is within 8mm of an available
delta. A 20mm gap with an A2-to-A3 swap lands at 2mm.

**What survives and carries the argument alone:** "what do I change?" is a
discrete search over a small set. The enumerable thing is the substitution,
not the tolerance. A floating-point tolerance model has nothing to search; it
widens a threshold until the verdict flips. That follows from closure being
integer equality over a finite parts list, and needs no statistic.

**Also withdrawn:** a 2,000-loop sample reporting 99.7% `EXCEEDS` with an
empty `NEEDS_GIVE` column. The generator splices straights into a closed ring,
so it opens a circuit by construction; the empty column describes the
generator, not layouts people draw. The incumbent tolerance defaults quoted in
an earlier draft are withdrawn as unsourced, the same defect T6 was corrected
