# Design: The joints bend: model angular give from the floor

## Problem Statement

The owner measured real BRIO track (CALIBRATION.md, `engine/data/measurements.ts`,
2026-10-30). The decisive reading is Q1: the loop E E E E D E E E E A has an exact
72mm gap, the app reports it EXCEEDS the give, and on the floor it **closes with every
joint seated (strained) and a train runs round it**.

The app is wrong in the direction that matters most: it tells people a working layout
will not close. The cause is known. `totalGiveMm` (`engine/src/solver/residual.ts`)
sums only axial give per joint (measured: 2mm pull, 0mm push, so 20mm for ten joints),
and `angularMaxDeg` is stored on every `Joint` and read nowhere. The graph path also
refuses every loop once any closing weld is rotated (`rotationBroken` in
`analyseCycles`, `engine/src/graph/cycles.ts`).

The loop does not close by stretching. It closes by each joint bending a little
sideways; over a 400mm-wide loop those small angles add up to centimetres.

### The Q1 readings, reconciled

`engine/data/measurements.ts` is the authoritative record; the sheet in CALIBRATION.md
is what was written on the floor. Two Q1 entries on the sheet were corrected by the
owner in this session, and step 1 records both corrections with this date in the
sheet (a dated note under Q1) and in `measurements.ts`'s `method`:

- "Does a train run round it without derailing? NO" meant **no, it did not derail**:
  the train runs. `trainRuns: true`.
- The "4mm" written under "if no, leftover gap" is void: the loop closed with every
  joint seated, so that box does not apply. `leftoverGapMm: null`.
- The Q2 bend box ("4 mm over 0 mm") was not measured; it stays null. The bend limit
  is derived from Q1 instead (Premise 2).

## What Makes This Cool

Every wooden-railway planner treats joint slack as length, if at all. The floor says it
is mostly angle, and angle grows with the size of the loop. A planner that models it can
say "this nearly-there loop will close" truthfully, where exact-length planners say no.
Crowd-sourced limits are a possible future, not a requirement of this design.

## Constraints

- Positions stay exact (the Z[zeta16] lattice); bend analysis is a separate
  floating-point layer that reads exact residuals and never feeds back into placement.
- In scope: the bend only. Out of scope by owner decision: the bridge clearance result
  (55mm train, -3mm) and the ramp's measured 225mm run. Their readings are committed
  as read; what the engine does with them is decided separately.
- The verdict copy keeps distinguishing the model from the world.

## Premises

1. The fix is to model sideways bend (angular give), not to raise the axial number: the
   loop closes a 72mm gap against 20mm of axial give, so no length figure explains it.
2. The bend limit is **derived from the Q1 loop**, which closed and ran. It is a lower
   bound on what real joints do while trains still run, recorded as *inferred from Q1*,
   not measured.
3. Bridges and the ramp are out of scope for this design.

Derived figure (bounded probe; becomes a committed test in step 2): the smallest
worst-joint bend that closes Q1, stretch excluded, is **2.99 degrees** (linearised LP,
exact to 0.01). Bends: -2.99, -0.35, 2.99, 2.99, 2.99, 2.99, 0.35, -2.99, -2.99, -2.99.
This 2.99 is the STRETCH-EXCLUDED probe figure, kept only as a test pin.

The limit the verdict uses includes the 0-2mm stretch. Probe with stretch: the LP puts
2mm on the four joints that lengthen the A side and gives 2.71 degrees linearised; an
exact re-walk with refinement settles at **about 2.8 degrees** (2.82 after a
minimum-norm refinement, leftover 0.00mm). LIMIT is whatever the shipped pipeline
returns for Q1, expected 2.7 to 2.8; every user-facing number below is LIMIT, never the
2.99 probe. The current estimate is 1.5 degrees.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Lever-arm bound
One total: per joint, stretch plus bend times its distance to the gap. Smallest diff,
keeps today's "definitely past the give" logic, but ignores heading closure and is loose.

### Approach B: Bend budget (chosen)
Compute the smallest worst-joint bend that closes the layout's loops, as a small linear
program over joint angles and stretches, and compare with the inferred limit.

### Approach C: Relaxed shape on the canvas
Whole-layout nonlinear solve with the floated shape drawn. Ruled out for now: a browser
solver far beyond "fix the engine on my numbers".

## Recommended Approach

Approach B.

```
 placement frames (exact) ──► per connected run: every fundamental loop walked
                               from the frames; its exact SE(2) residual (x, y, heading)
                                   │
                                   ▼
          one LP per connected run:  variables = one bend t_w and one stretch s_w
          per weld (each weld ONCE, shared by every loop through it);
          3 equalities per fundamental loop (linearised closure);
          |t_w| <= lambda * limit_w,  0 <= s_w <= 2mm;   minimise lambda
                                   │
                                   ▼
          re-walk exactly; up to 3 refinements, each re-solving the LP
          linearised at the current solution
                                   │
              lambda, leftover  ──► verdict rule (below)
```

### The verdict rule (replaces the axial comparison)

- Per connected run: **closes within the modelled give iff lambda <= 1.001 AND the
  exact leftover after refinement is <= 0.5mm.** lambda is dimensionless (needed bend
  as a fraction of each weld's own limit), so mixed BRIO and third-party runs compare
  in one unit. For an all-BRIO run the needed bend in degrees is lambda x LIMIT,
  displayed to 0.1 degrees.
- `totalGiveMm` is retired from the verdict; stretch lives inside the LP as the
  0-2mm per-joint variables. Its necessary-condition docblock is replaced by this rule.
- Copy, two tiers, never a claim that track will not fit:
  - within (all BRIO): "Closes: needs 2.4° of bend per joint at most; real joints were
    shown to bend {LIMIT}°."
  - beyond (all BRIO): "Needs 4.1° of bend per joint, beyond what real joints were
    shown to do ({LIMIT}°). It may still close on the floor." This replaces "exceeds
    the modelled give". The limit is a LOWER bound, so above it the app makes no
    failure claim.
  - mixed run (any weld touching a third-party part): no single degree figure exists,
    so the figure is lambda as a percentage of each joint's own limit, and the copy
    lists EVERY third-party part kind in the run with its limit and status, sorted by
    part id. No weld is singled out as binding (that is not unique). Examples:
    within: "Closes: needs 80% of each joint's bend. BRIO joints were shown to bend
    {LIMIT}°; joints to IKEA C45 can bend an estimated 1.5°."
    beyond: "Needs 130% of each joint's bend. BRIO joints were shown to bend
    {LIMIT}°; joints to IKEA C45 can bend an estimated 1.5°. It may still close on
    the floor."
    An estimated third-party bound is never described as shown by real joints. The
    order-independence test covers this copy.
- If refinement leaves more than 0.5mm, the run reads "beyond", with the leftover in mm.
- Heading: a whole-step mismatch (22.5° and up) is just a large residual in the
  heading row; the LP and the refinement handle it, and the leftover rule guards the
  linearisation. No separate heading gate remains.

### Report fields

- The verdict is run-level only. The run result carries `lambda`, `neededBendDeg`
  (all-BRIO runs) and `leftoverMm`; lambda is unique even when the optimal bends are
  not, so it is order-independent.
- Per loop, `CycleReport` gains `standaloneLambda`: that loop's own min-max solved
  alone, a deterministic diagnostic that ranks `worst`. It never decides the verdict.
  `withinGive` is removed from `CycleReport`; the run-level rule replaces it.
- No "worst joints" list in v1: which welds bind is not unique across optimal
  solutions, so naming them would depend on solver order.

### Where the data comes from

- Graph path (the one the app uses for every verdict): `analyseCycles` already has the
  placement frames and the traced loops. Each fundamental loop is walked from those
  frames to get its joint poses and its own exact SE(2) residual; gaps are never
  summed across rotated loops. The global `rotationBroken` gate is removed.
- The chain path (`computeResidual`) is used by engine tests and `bun run
  calibration`; it builds the same one-loop input and calls the same LP, so Q1 is
  checked identically on both paths.
- Cost: one LP per connected run, at most 3 x rank equalities over its welds; rank is
  already capped by `CYCLE_RANK_CEILING`, and the LP is charged against
  `CYCLE_WORK_BUDGET` by its weld count.

### Shared joints

Loops that share welds are solved in ONE LP (the whole connected run), each weld one
variable, so no joint's bend is spent twice. This keeps cycles.ts rule 2, "give is
never pooled".

### The limit: one value, defined by the solver

- `LIMIT` is not typed in. `engine/data/calibration.ts` derives it at load by running
  the shipped LP (stretch included, 0-2mm) on Q1 when `q1.closes` and `q1.trainRuns`
  are true. It is the solver's own output, so Q1 is never on the wrong side of it by
  rounding or a model mismatch.
- `calibrationStatus` gains `'inferred'`. Precedence: a valid Q2 bend reading
  (`measured`) wins; else the Q1-inferred value (`inferred`); else the 1.5° estimate
  (`estimated`).
- One global limit for BRIO-to-BRIO welds. A weld touching a third-party part keeps
  that part's own `slop.angularDeg` (still estimated) as its `limit_w`; lambda is the
  common scale on all of them.
- LIMIT is the refined result of the same pipeline the verdict runs (LP plus
  refinement). If Q1's own refined leftover exceeds 0.5mm, derivation falls back to
  the estimate and a test fails loudly.
- `findings()` / `bun run calibration` stop printing "the engine reads no angular
  give today" and print the inferred limit with its source.

## Success Criteria

- Q1 reads as closing (lambda = 1 within 0.001), and reads "beyond" if LIMIT is
  lowered by 0.1 degrees (spec pin).
- The probe's figure is a committed test: Q1's linearised min-max bend, stretch
  excluded, is 2.99 ± 0.01 degrees.
- A loop needing clearly more bend than the limit reads "beyond", with no failure claim.
- Exactly closing layouts are unchanged (differential over the existing fixture corpus).
- Order-independent: cyclic reorderings and reversed traversal give the same verdict.
- Two loops sharing a weld never both spend its full bend (a constructed case).
- The leftover after refinement is reported for every run the app calls closing, and
  is <= 0.5mm by rule.
