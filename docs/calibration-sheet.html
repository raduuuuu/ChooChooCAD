F4 · How far can raised track go between two supports?

The app will check that raised track has a support close enough. "Close enough" is this one figure. Two straights joined over the gap, the joint in the middle, is the hardest case, so measure that.

Use two of whatever holds your raised track up: track supports, risers, or two equal stacks of books. Rest two medium straights (A, 144) on them, joined in the middle. Start with the supports close, then slide them apart a little at a time, keeping the joint in the middle. At each step, lay a ruler along the top of the track across the joint: stop when the joint dips more than 2mm under the ruler, or when the train, pushed across by hand, catches at the joint. Measure the last gap that held.

S2 · The arch over the lift shaft (2026-11-07)

Seen from the back, the green panel between the lift's two posts has an arch cut in its bottom edge. The app puts the top of that arch at 154 from the table; the back photo puts it lower, with a band of green between it and the ledge under the red wheel. Three ruler readings settle it.

Turn the crank until the lift car is at the bottom. For A1 and A2, stand the ruler upright on the top of the base, inside the shaft, and read where the arch's underside crosses it: A1 under the middle of the arch, where it is highest, and A2 against a post, where the curve stops. If the panel is in the way of your eye, lay a card flat under the arch and read the ruler at the card. A0 is the base's own thickness, at its edge.
